Stellantis' PureTech 1.2 and 1.6 engines (Peugeot, Citroën, DS, Opel) have equipped millions of vehicles since 2014. Praised for their efficiency and elected "Engine of the Year," they are now at the heart of a global controversy concerning the reliability of their oil-bath timing belt. In this exhaustive guide, I dissect the real problems, extended warranties, and give you all the keys to monitor and maintain your PureTech.

## The central problem: The oil-bath timing belt

### What is an oil-bath belt?

Unlike conventional timing belts in the open air, PureTech engines use a belt immersed in engine oil. This technology, developed to reduce friction and extend theoretical lifespan, poses in practice problems of premature degradation.

### Observed failures

**Accelerated degradation**: The toothed belt, subjected to high oil temperatures (90-110°C) and mechanical stress, decomposes prematurely. Rubber particles detach and contaminate the lubrication circuit.

**Catastrophic consequences**: When the belt breaks or skips several teeth, the valves come into contact with the pistons. The engine is then destroyed and requires a complete replacement (cost: €8,000 to €15,000 depending on model).

### Timeline of the scandal

- **2014-2018**: PureTech commercialization, no reported problems
- **2019-2020**: First failures reported (60,000-90,000 km)
- **2021**: Multiplication of cases, class actions launched
- **2022**: Stellantis acknowledges the problem, extended warranty announced
- **2023**: SToA (Specific Technical Operation Action) program deployed
- **2024-2026**: 10-year/200,000 km warranty extension for timing system

## Warning symptoms: Detecting the problem before breakage

### Early symptoms (immediate action required)

**Engine light on**: Camshaft sensor error (P0016, P0017, P0018). Timing calibration is offset.

**Unstable idle**: Engine hunts, oscillates between 600 and 900 rpm when stopped.

**Difficult starting**: Several attempts necessary, especially when cold.

**Progressive power loss**: Sluggish acceleration, impression of "running out of breath."

**Metallic clicking**: "Grinding" noise at idle, sign of degraded belt.

### Advanced symptoms (absolute emergency)

**Unexpected stalling**: Engine cuts while driving.

**Limp mode**: Orange light + "Engine fault, repair vehicle" message.

**Metal rattling at startup**: Metal-on-metal contact, sign of skipped timing.

**⚠️ DANGER**: Stop the vehicle immediately. Call tow truck. Do not drive anymore. Risk of engine destruction in a few kilometers.

## Stellantis SToA warranty: What does it really cover?

### Extended warranty details

**Duration**: 10 years from date of first registration OR 200,000 km (whichever comes first).

**Scope**: 
- Oil-bath timing belt
- Belt tensioner
- Camshaft sprocket
- Front crankshaft oil seal
- Labor included

**Conditions**:
✅ Maintenance according to manufacturer recommendations (stamped service book)
✅ Use of PSA B71 2290 approved oil (0W30)
✅ Strict oil change every 15,000 km or 1 year

### How to activate the SToA warranty?

1. **Appointment**: Peugeot/Citroën/DS/Opel dealership
2. **Diagnosis**: Diagnostic tool reading + visual inspection
3. **SToA activation**: Manufacturer file declaration
4. **Preventive replacement**: Belt + tensioner + oil changed (4h operation, free under warranty)

**Important**: The warranty does not activate automatically. You must justify maintenance and request SToA application. In case of dealership refusal, write to Stellantis consumer service (registered mail).

## Preventive maintenance: Monitoring protocol

### Specific PureTech oil change

**MANDATORY interval**: 15,000 km or 12 months (whichever comes first).

**MANDATORY oil**: PSA B71 2290 specification (0W30 ACEA C2).

**Compatible brands**:
- Total Quartz Ineo MC3 0W30
- Castrol Edge Professional LL03 0W30
- Mobil 1 ESP Formula 0W30
- Motul 8100 X-clean+ 0W30

**Quantity**: 3.75L (1.2L) / 4.25L (1.6L)

**⚠️ WARNING**: NEVER use classic 5W30 or 5W40 oil. Incorrect viscosity accelerates belt degradation.

### Regular visual inspection

**Every 5,000 km**:
- Oil level: Always between min and max
- Oil color: Golden/amber. If opaque black, early oil change
- Possible leak: Inspection under engine
- Idle stability: Must be constant 750-800 rpm

**Every 15,000 km (during oil change)**:
- Ask the mechanic: "Oil-bath belt visual inspection"
- Belt photo: Must be smooth, without cracks, without visible particles
- Fault reading: Diagnostic tool, suspicious P001X codes

### Preventive replacement outside warranty

If your vehicle has > 5 years and > 100,000 km, consider preventive replacement.

**Operation cost**: €800 to €1,200 (dealership) / €500 to €800 (qualified independent garage)

**Parts**:
- Timing belt: €180-250
- Tensioner: €120-180
- Seal kit: €50-80
- 0W30 oil: €40-60

**Intervention duration**: 4-5 hours

**Profitability**: €1,000 preventive vs €10,000 engine rebuild. Obvious calculation.

## FAQ: Your questions about PureTech

**My PureTech has 140,000 km without problems, am I out of the woods?**

Not totally. Some engines break at 180,000 km. Continue strict maintenance, monitor symptoms. You're in the 94% of non-failing engines, but vigilance remains necessary.

**Can I do maintenance at an independent garage?**

Yes, BUT require PSA B71 2290 oil and detailed invoices. In case of problem, Stellantis may refuse warranty if non-compliant non-network maintenance. Favor dealership for traceability.

**Does the SToA warranty cover collateral damage?**

No. If the belt breaks and destroys the engine, only the belt replacement is covered. The rest (cylinder head, pistons) is at your expense except favorable case law (15% of cases).

**Are new 2025-2026 PureTech corrected?**

Stellantis has made modifications (reinforced belt, specific oil from factory) on engines assembled after mid-2024. Insufficient hindsight to confirm definitive resolution.

## Why is the PEA still relevant in 2026?

The PEA retains its major tax advantages: after 5 years of ownership, your capital gains are exempt from income tax (only social contributions of 17.2% apply). This is considerably more advantageous than an ordinary securities account subject to the 30% flat tax.

### Key PEA figures for 2026

- **Contribution ceiling**: €150,000 (standard PEA)
- **Tax advantage**: Total exemption from capital gains tax after 5 years
- **Average historical return**: 8-10% annually on broad indices
- **Management fees**: < 0.30% on the best ETFs

## Mistakes to avoid in 2026

After supporting dozens of investors, I've identified the most common pitfalls:

1. **Withdrawing before 5 years**: You lose all tax advantages
2. **Over-diversifying**: 3 to 5 ETFs are sufficient for optimal diversification
3. **Market timing**: Regular investing (DCA) beats 90% of timing strategies
4. **Neglecting fees**: 0.5% extra fees = -15% capital over 30 years

## Tax optimisation: the latest 2026 rules

PEA taxation hasn't changed, but a few points deserve attention:

- Dividends from foreign shares eligible for the PEA are subject to withholding tax in the issuing country (non-recoverable)
- Favour accumulating ETFs rather than distributing ones to maximise compound interest effects
- PEA transfers between banks are now free at most brokers
